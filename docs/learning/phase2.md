## 0. What this phase computes

tmfem phase 2 computes the displacement and stress field in a 2D body under in-plane loads. The body
is either a thin plate (plane stress) or a long prism loaded the same way along its length (plane
strain). Loads are surface tractions on boundary edges, and displacements can be imposed on edges or
points.

It uses linear (T3) and quadratic isoparametric (T6) triangles on meshes generated by Gmsh.

Every capability is checked against a closed-form answer:
- the patch test (a linear displacement field must be reproduced exactly)
- a uniform bar in tension
- the Kirsch plate with a circular hole: the stress concentration factor, point stresses and the
  observed orders of convergence

## 1. Strong form

Unknown: the displacement `u(x) = (u_x, u_y)` in metres. Small strain, linear isotropic material:

| Equation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `div sigma + b = 0` in Ω | equilibrium (b = body force per volume, N/m3) |
| `eps = ½ (grad u + grad uᵀ)` | small-strain kinematics |
| `sigma = C : eps` | Hooke's law (E, ν) |
| `u = g` on Γ_u | imposed displacement |
| `sigma n = t̄` on Γ_t | imposed traction (Pa), n is the outward unit normal |

Γ_u and Γ_t together make up the boundary, and do not overlap in the same component. A boundary
with neither condition is traction-free (`t̄ = 0`), like the hole.

Sign convention: tension is positive.

## 2. Weak form (principle of virtual work)

Take a test field w (a "virtual displacement") that vanishes wherever u is imposed. Take the dot
product of the equilibrium equation with w and integrate over the body:

$$\int_\Omega w\cdot(\operatorname{div}\sigma)\,dV + \int_\Omega w\cdot b\,dV = 0.$$

Use the divergence theorem on `div(sigma w) = w·div(sigma) + sigma : grad w`:

$$\int_\Omega w\cdot(\operatorname{div}\sigma)\,dV = \int_{\partial\Omega} w\cdot(\sigma n)\,dA - \int_\Omega \sigma : \operatorname{grad} w\,dV.$$

- sigma is symmetric, so only the symmetric part of `grad w` survives: `sigma : grad w = sigma : eps(w)`.
- On Γ_u, w = 0, so the unknown reaction tractions never appear.
- On Γ_t, `sigma n = t̄`.

The weak form: find u with u = g on Γ_u such that, for every admissible w,

$$\int_\Omega \varepsilon(w) : C : \varepsilon(u)\,dV = \int_\Omega w\cdot b\,dV + \int_{\Gamma_t} w\cdot\bar t\,dA.$$

Left: internal virtual work. Right: external virtual work. Only first derivatives appear, so
continuous piecewise polynomials are admissible.

**2D reduction.** Every quantity is independent of z. Then `dV = t dA` and `dA = t ds`, with t the
thickness: the plate thickness in plane stress, a slice of the prism in plane strain. tmfem uses
**t = 1 m** in both cases. So forces and reactions are in N per metre of depth (N/m), while stresses
and displacements are unaffected.

Code: `src/mechanics/elastic2d.cpp`.

## 3. Voigt notation

Store the symmetric 2D tensors as 3-vectors:

$$\boldsymbol\varepsilon = \begin{bmatrix}\varepsilon_{xx}\\ \varepsilon_{yy}\\ \gamma_{xy}\end{bmatrix},\quad \gamma_{xy} = 2\varepsilon_{xy} = \frac{\partial u_x}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial u_y}{\partial x},\qquad
\boldsymbol\sigma = \begin{bmatrix}\sigma_{xx}\\ \sigma_{yy}\\ \sigma_{xy}\end{bmatrix}.$$

**Why engineering shear (the factor 2).** The double contraction has two equal shear terms:
`sigma : eps = s_xx e_xx + s_yy e_yy + 2 s_xy e_xy`. With `gamma = 2 e_xy` this is exactly the
dot product `sigmaᵀ eps`, so energy and virtual work need no correction factor. The price is that
the shear entry of D is G = μ, not 2μ.

**The B matrix.** With `u_h = Σ_a N_a(x) u_a` and dofs interleaved as `(u_x, u_y)` per node
(dof `2a + c`):

$$\boldsymbol\varepsilon = B\,\mathbf u_e,\qquad B_a = \begin{bmatrix}\partial_x N_a & 0\\ 0 & \partial_y N_a\\ \partial_y N_a & \partial_x N_a\end{bmatrix},\qquad B = [B_1\ B_2\ \cdots\ B_n]\quad(3\times 2n).$$

Code: `src/mechanics/elasticity.cpp` (`strain_displacement`), `src/fem/assembly.cpp` (dof numbering).

## 4. D matrices for plane stress and plane strain

Start from 3D isotropic Hooke's law in compliance form, with the out-of-plane shears zero in both cases:

$$\varepsilon_{xx} = \tfrac{1}{E}[\sigma_{xx} - \nu(\sigma_{yy}+\sigma_{zz})],\quad
\varepsilon_{yy} = \tfrac{1}{E}[\sigma_{yy} - \nu(\sigma_{xx}+\sigma_{zz})],\quad
\varepsilon_{zz} = \tfrac{1}{E}[\sigma_{zz} - \nu(\sigma_{xx}+\sigma_{yy})],\quad
\gamma_{xy} = \tfrac{2(1+\nu)}{E}\sigma_{xy}.$$

**Plane stress** (thin plate, free faces): `sigma_zz = 0`. Invert the 2×2 in-plane block:

$$D_{\text{ps}} = \frac{E}{1-\nu^2}\begin{bmatrix}1 & \nu & 0\\ \nu & 1 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & \tfrac{1-\nu}{2}\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\varepsilon_{zz} = -\frac{\nu}{E}(\sigma_{xx}+\sigma_{yy}) = -\frac{\nu}{1-\nu}(\varepsilon_{xx}+\varepsilon_{yy}).$$

**Plane strain** (long body, ends held): `eps_zz = 0`. The third equation gives
`sigma_zz = ν (sigma_xx + sigma_yy)`. Substituting:

$$D_{\text{pe}} = \frac{E}{(1+\nu)(1-2\nu)}\begin{bmatrix}1-\nu & \nu & 0\\ \nu & 1-\nu & 0\\ 0 & 0 & \tfrac{1-2\nu}{2}\end{bmatrix}.$$

The shear entry in both is `E/(2(1+ν)) = G` (check: `E(1−ν)/(2(1−ν²)) = E/(2(1+ν))` and
`E(1−2ν)/(2(1+ν)(1−2ν)) = E/(2(1+ν))`).

**Unit check used in the tests.** Plane strain with (E, ν) equals plane stress with
`E* = E/(1−ν²)`, `ν* = ν/(1−ν)`. Then `1 − ν*² = (1−2ν)/(1−ν)²`, so
`E*/(1−ν*²) = E(1−ν)/((1+ν)(1−2ν))`, which is the (1,1) entry of D_pe.

**Kolosov constant** κ, which appears in the Kirsch displacement:
- plane stress: `κ = (3−ν)/(1+ν)`
- plane strain: `κ = 3 − 4ν`

**von Mises stress** (3D formula, with the out-of-plane normal stress included):

$$\sigma_{vm} = \sqrt{\tfrac12\left[(\sigma_{xx}-\sigma_{yy})^2 + (\sigma_{yy}-\sigma_{zz})^2 + (\sigma_{zz}-\sigma_{xx})^2\right] + 3\sigma_{xy}^2},$$

with `sigma_zz = 0` in plane stress and `ν (sigma_xx + sigma_yy)` in plane strain.

Code: `src/mechanics/elasticity.cpp` (`elasticity_matrix`, `kappa`, `sigma_zz`, `von_mises`).

## 5. Shape functions and the isoparametric map

Reference triangle with vertices (0,0), (1,0), (0,1) and area coordinates
`L1 = 1 − ξ − η`, `L2 = ξ`, `L3 = η`.

**T3** (3 nodes, linear): `N_a = L_a`.

**T6** (6 nodes, quadratic). Gmsh order: corners 0, 1, 2, then mid-sides 3 (edge 0-1), 4 (edge 1-2),
5 (edge 2-0):

$$N_0 = L_1(2L_1-1),\ N_1 = L_2(2L_2-1),\ N_2 = L_3(2L_3-1),\quad N_3 = 4L_1L_2,\ N_4 = 4L_2L_3,\ N_5 = 4L_3L_1.$$

Each N_a is 1 at its own node and 0 at the other five (for example, a corner function vanishes at the
mid-sides because `2L − 1 = 0` at L = ½). They sum to 1:
`Σ L_a(2L_a − 1) + 4(L1L2 + L2L3 + L3L1) = 2(L1+L2+L3)² − (L1+L2+L3) = 1`.

**Boundary edges** use the 1D versions on ξ ∈ [−1, 1]. Gmsh order is ends first, then the mid node:
- Line2: `N = ((1−ξ)/2, (1+ξ)/2)`
- Line3: `N = (ξ(ξ−1)/2, ξ(ξ+1)/2, 1−ξ²)`

**Isoparametric map.** The same functions interpolate the geometry and the displacement:

$$\mathbf x(\xi,\eta) = \sum_a N_a(\xi,\eta)\,\mathbf x_a,\qquad
J = \frac{\partial \mathbf x}{\partial(\xi,\eta)} = \sum_a \mathbf x_a \otimes \nabla_\xi N_a,\qquad
\nabla_x N_a = J^{-T}\nabla_\xi N_a,\qquad dA = \det J\,d\xi\,d\eta.$$

- For T3 and straight-sided T6, J is constant.
- When a T6 has a mid-side node off the chord, J varies, and the element edge is a parabola through
  the three nodes.
- `det J > 0` is required. Gmsh may write triangles clockwise, so the reader reorders any
  clockwise triangle (swap corners 1↔2 and mid-sides 3↔5). The geometry code throws if det J ≤ 0.

**Why the hole needs curved T6 edges.** Gmsh's second-order meshes place mid-side nodes on the true
geometry, so each hole edge is a parabola that matches the circle to O(h³). A straight-sided T6
would replace the arc by chords: an O(h²) geometric error that the quadratic field cannot fix.
The Strang-Berger estimate then caps the energy error at O(h^{3/2}) instead of O(h²).

**Why the patch test is a real test even on distorted meshes.** Choose `u = A x + c` (linear).
Since `Σ N_a = 1` and `Σ N_a x_a = x`, the interpolant `Σ N_a (A x_a + c) = A x + c` equals the exact
field everywhere in the element, for ANY node positions with `det J > 0`. So the exact solution lies
in the FE space, and Galerkin with exact integration must return it exactly.

**With quadrature there is one more condition.** The internal force at node a for a constant stress
σ is `∫ B_aᵀ σ dA = Σ_q B_aᵀ σ det J w_q`. Equilibrium of a constant stress needs this sum to equal the
exact integral, which reduces to a boundary integral of `σ n N_a`. The integrand is
`det J ∇_x N_a = adj(J)ᵀ ∇_ξ N_a`.
- For a curved T6, J is linear in ξ, so adj(J) is linear. ∇_ξ N_a is linear too, so the product has
  degree 2, and the 3-point rule integrates it exactly. That is why the curved-T6 patch test passes to
  round-off (8e-14).
- With a 1-point rule, a curved T6 would fail the patch test.
- For T3 and straight T6, J is constant, so the condition is weaker.

The patch test checks the parts that this argument does not cover:
- assembly and dof numbering
- the B matrix, D and the quadrature
- edge tractions and Dirichlet elimination

Code: `src/fem/shape_functions.cpp`, `src/fem/element_geometry.cpp`.

## 6. Quadrature

| Use | Rule | Exact for |
|---|---|---|
| T3 stiffness | 1 point (1/3, 1/3), w = 1/2 | degree 1 (BᵀDB is constant) |
| T6 stiffness | 3 points (1/6, 1/6), (2/3, 1/6), (1/6, 2/3), w = 1/6 | degree 2 (BᵀDB is quadratic for straight T6) |
| Error norms | collapsed Gauss, 4 × 4 = 16 points | degree 6 |
| Edge tractions | 5-point Gauss-Legendre on [−1, 1] | degree 9 |

**Curved T6 elements.** BᵀDB is rational, so no rule is exact. Ciarlet's theory says a rule exact
for degree 2p − 2 (= 2 for p = 2) keeps the optimal convergence rates, and the 3-point rule meets it.

**Collapsed (Duffy) rule.** It maps the square [0,1]² onto the triangle with `ξ = u`, `η = v(1−u)`,
so `dξ dη = (1−u) du dv`:

$$\int_T f\,d\xi\,d\eta = \int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1 f(u,\,v(1-u))\,(1-u)\,du\,dv \approx \sum_{i,j} w_i w_j (1-u_i)\, f(u_i,\,v_j(1-u_i)).$$

- `u_i` and `v_j` are the n-point Gauss-Legendre points mapped to [0,1], with weights halved.
- The monomial `ξ^p η^q` becomes `u^p (1−u)^{q+1} v^q`: degree p+q+1 in u and q in v. Gauss with
  n points integrates degree 2n−1 in each variable, so the rule is exact for total degree
  p + q ≤ 2n − 2. With n = 4 that is degree 6 (16 points).
- It reuses the existing `gauss_legendre(n)`, so there is no table of magic numbers to mistype.
- The unit test checks `∫ ξ^p η^q = p! q! / (p+q+2)!` for every p + q up to the claimed degree.

**Edge tractions.** The Kirsch tractions are not polynomial. With 5 points, the quadrature error on an
edge of length h is O(h^10), far below the discretisation error.

Code: `src/fem/quadrature.cpp` (`triangle_rule`, `triangle_collapsed_gauss`).

## 7. Element matrices, loads and assembly

Insert `u_h = N u_e` and `w = N w_e` into the weak form. It must hold for every w_e, which gives
`K u = f`:

$$K_e = \int_{\Omega_e} B^T D B\,dA \approx \sum_q B(\xi_q)^T D\, B(\xi_q)\,\det J_q\,w_q\qquad(2n\times 2n)$$

$$f_e = \int_{\Gamma_e} N^T \bar{\mathbf t}\,ds \approx \sum_q N(\xi_q)^T\,\bar{\mathbf t}(\mathbf x(\xi_q))\,\left|\frac{d\mathbf x}{d\xi}\right|_q w_q,$$

- On an edge, `ds = |dx/dξ| dξ`, which accounts for a curved Line3 edge automatically.
- N on an edge is the 2 × 2n_edge matrix that interleaves the scalar functions.
- Body force is not used in phase 2.

**Assembly.**
- Each element's K_e entries are pushed as triplets `(dof_i, dof_j, value)` in element order, so the
  result is deterministic.
- `Eigen::SparseMatrix::setFromTriplets` sums duplicates.
- K is symmetric. It is positive semi-definite with a 3D null space (two translations and one
  rotation), since rigid motions produce no strain.

**Dirichlet by elimination** (phase 1 section 6, same `DirichletPartition` class):

$$K_{ff}\,\mathbf u_f = \mathbf f_f - K_{fc}\,\mathbf g,\qquad \mathbf r_c = K_{cf}\,\mathbf u_f + K_{cc}\,\mathbf g - \mathbf f_c.$$

- `r_c` are the reaction forces (N/m) at the constrained dofs.
- K_ff is symmetric positive definite once the constraints remove all three rigid modes, so it is
  solved with `Eigen::SimplicialLDLT`.
- If the constraints miss a rigid mode, K_ff is singular. LDLT does not fail cleanly: it still reports
  success, and the zero pivot comes out as round-off (about 1e-15 of the largest, possibly positive).
  So the solver compares every pivot with the largest one and throws below 1e-10. A test removes the
  bar's u_y support and checks that the solver throws.

Code: `src/fem/assembly.cpp`, `src/mechanics/elastic2d.cpp`, `src/fem/dirichlet.cpp`.

## 8. Stress recovery

The FE stress `sigma_h = D B(ξ) u_e` is discontinuous across elements. It is constant per T3 and linear
per straight T6. Three uses:

1. **Gauss-point stresses.** These are where the stiffness was integrated. Phase 3 stores history there.
2. **Nodal averages, for VTU files and plots only.** Each node gets the unweighted mean of
   `D B(ξ_node) u_e` over the elements that contain it. This smooths the field for viewing, but it is
   not used for any validation number.
3. **Point values, for validation.** For a point x:
   - find every element that contains it: invert `x(ξ) = x` by Newton,
     `ξ ← ξ + J(ξ)⁻¹ (x − x(ξ))`, which is exact in one step for T3
   - an element contains the point if `ξ ≥ −ε`, `η ≥ −ε`, `1 − ξ − η ≥ −ε` (ε = 1e-10)
   - take `D B(ξ) u_e` in each such element, and average if the point lies on a shared edge or node
   - this definition is fixed before seeing any result. It matters at the Kt point (a, 0), which is a
     mesh node shared by one or two elements.

Code: `src/mechanics/stress_recovery.cpp`, `src/fem/element_geometry.cpp` (`inverse_map`).

## 9. The Kirsch plate and exact boundary tractions

**Setup.** An infinite plate has a circular hole of radius a. A remote uniaxial stress S acts along y.
Following the source, θ is measured **from the load axis +y toward +x**:

$$x = r\sin\theta,\quad y = r\cos\theta,\qquad \mathbf e_r = (\sin\theta,\ \cos\theta),\quad \mathbf e_\theta = \frac{\partial \mathbf e_r}{\partial\theta} = (\cos\theta,\ -\sin\theta).$$

The polar components are `σ_rr = e_rᵀ σ e_r`, `σ_θθ = e_θᵀ σ e_θ` and `σ_rθ = e_rᵀ σ e_θ`.

**Polar stresses** (Kirsch, 1898):

$$\sigma_{rr} = \tfrac S2\left(1-\tfrac{a^2}{r^2}\right) + \tfrac S2\left(1 - 4\tfrac{a^2}{r^2} + 3\tfrac{a^4}{r^4}\right)\cos 2\theta$$

$$\sigma_{\theta\theta} = \tfrac S2\left(1+\tfrac{a^2}{r^2}\right) - \tfrac S2\left(1 + 3\tfrac{a^4}{r^4}\right)\cos 2\theta$$

$$\sigma_{r\theta} = -\tfrac S2\left(1 + 2\tfrac{a^2}{r^2} - 3\tfrac{a^4}{r^4}\right)\sin 2\theta$$

Checks:
- At r = a: `σ_rr = σ_rθ = 0` (free hole). `σ_θθ = S(1 − 2 cos 2θ)`, which is −S at θ = 0 and 3S at
  θ = 90°. So **Kt = 3**.
- As r → ∞: `σ_rr → S cos²θ` and `σ_θθ → S sin²θ`. With θ measured from the load axis, this is the
  uniaxial field.

**Cartesian stresses.** The textbook formulas are written for load along X with θ from X:

$$\sigma_{XX} = S\left[1 - \tfrac{a^2}{r^2}\left(\tfrac32\cos2\theta + \cos4\theta\right) + \tfrac32\tfrac{a^4}{r^4}\cos4\theta\right]$$

$$\sigma_{YY} = S\left[-\tfrac{a^2}{r^2}\left(\tfrac12\cos2\theta - \cos4\theta\right) - \tfrac32\tfrac{a^4}{r^4}\cos4\theta\right]$$

$$\sigma_{XY} = S\left[-\tfrac{a^2}{r^2}\left(\tfrac12\sin2\theta + \sin4\theta\right) + \tfrac32\tfrac{a^4}{r^4}\sin4\theta\right]$$

The model has the load along y. Relabel `X = y`, `Y = x`, so `θ = atan2(x, y)`, which is the angle
from +y toward +x, as required. A relabelling of axes is an orthogonal map (a reflection). Stress is a
tensor, so its components simply swap:

$$\sigma_{yy} = \sigma_{XX},\qquad \sigma_{xx} = \sigma_{YY},\qquad \sigma_{xy} = \sigma_{XY}.$$

The same relabelling also explains why `e_θ = (cos θ, −sin θ)`: in (X, Y) it is the usual
`(−sin θ, cos θ)`.

I checked this numerically: projecting the relabelled Cartesian tensor onto `e_r` and `e_θ`
reproduces the CSV rows (1.5a, 25°), (2a, 50°), (a, 90°) and (4a, 0°) to 4 decimals, including the
sign of σ_rθ. The unit test `kirsch_analytic.cpp` repeats this for every row.

**Exact displacement**, with shear modulus `μ = E/(2(1+ν))` and κ from section 4:

$$u_r = \frac{S}{4\mu}\left[r\left(\frac{\kappa-1}{2} + \cos2\theta\right) + \frac{a^2}{r}\big(1 + (1+\kappa)\cos2\theta\big) - \frac{a^4}{r^3}\cos2\theta\right]$$

$$u_\theta = \frac{S}{4\mu}\left[(1-\kappa)\frac{a^2}{r} - r - \frac{a^4}{r^3}\right]\sin2\theta$$

`u = u_r e_r + u_θ e_θ`, so `u_x = u_r sin θ + u_θ cos θ` and `u_y = u_r cos θ − u_θ sin θ`.

Checks:
- **a = 0** must give the uniaxial field `eps_yy = S/E`, `eps_xx = −νS/E` in plane stress. Then
  `u_r = r(eps_yy cos²θ + eps_xx sin²θ)`. The r-terms give `S(1+ν)/(2E) · r((κ−1)/2 + cos 2θ)` with
  `(κ−1)/2 = (1−ν)/(1+ν)`, which is `r S/E [(1−ν)/2 + (1+ν)/2 cos 2θ]`. ✓
- **Full field:** applying `sigma = D eps(u)` to the formulas above (central differences in polar
  coordinates) reproduces the Kirsch stresses to 4e-10 relative, in both plane conditions. This was
  checked numerically before writing this section, and `kirsch_analytic.cpp` repeats it in C++.
- **Symmetry:** `u_θ ∝ sin 2θ = 0` on θ = 0 (the y axis, so u_x = 0) and θ = 90° (the x axis, so
  u_y = 0). So the exact field satisfies the symmetry conditions with **no rigid-body term to fix**,
  and P2-f can compare u directly.

**The quarter model.** The geometry is a full square plate of side 10a with the hole at the centre. By
symmetry, only the quarter [0, 5a]² minus the hole is modelled. Its boundary conditions:

| Physical group | Edge | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| `sym_x` | x = 0, a ≤ y ≤ 5a | u_x = 0 (y-axis is a mirror plane) |
| `sym_y` | y = 0, a ≤ x ≤ 5a | u_y = 0 |
| `hole` | r = a | traction-free |
| `outer_right` | x = 5a, n = (1, 0) | `t̄ = σ_exact n = (σ_xx, σ_xy)` |
| `outer_top` | y = 5a, n = (0, 1) | `t̄ = σ_exact n = (σ_xy, σ_yy)` |

Why the symmetry conditions are right: on a mirror plane the normal displacement and the shear
traction must both vanish. The shear traction vanishes naturally, since a "free" component in the
weak form means zero traction in that component.

**Why exact tractions make the finite model exact.** The Kirsch field satisfies, inside the quarter:
- equilibrium and compatibility (it is the infinite-plate solution restricted to it)
- the free hole
- the symmetry conditions (shown above)
- by construction, the outer tractions `σ_exact n`

Linear elasticity with these boundary conditions has a unique solution. So the Kirsch field IS the
exact solution of the finite problem, and the FE solution converges to it with no truncation error.

With a uniform 140 MPa traction on the top edge instead, the perturbation terms at r = 5a are of order
`S a²/r² · 3/2 ≈ 8 MPa`. That would make the finite plate a different problem and break the 2 MPa
tolerance at r = 4a.

**Plane condition.** The Kirsch stresses contain no material constant. In 2D with no body force,
equilibrium plus compatibility reduce to `∇⁴φ = 0` for the Airy stress function φ, which has no
elastic constant in it. Michell's condition settles the hole: on a multiply-connected domain the
stresses are still free of E and ν when the traction on each hole has zero resultant force. The hole
here is traction-free, so the condition holds. So plane stress and plane strain give the same
stresses. Only the displacements differ, through κ and μ. P2-c..e use plane stress (a thin plate,
like the source's Abaqus model), and P2-f checks both conditions.

**Mapping CSV rows into the quarter.** The CSV lists θ from 0 to 360°. The field is symmetric about
both axes:
- σ_rr and σ_θθ are even in reflections.
- σ_rθ changes sign under each reflection, because a reflection reverses the direction of increasing θ.

| CSV θ | θ' in the quarter | σ_rθ |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 90° | θ | same |
| (90°, 180°] | 180° − θ | flipped |
| (180°, 270°] | θ − 180° | same (two reflections) |
| (270°, 360°] | 360° − θ | flipped |

Example: CSV (2a, 310°) has σ_rθ = +90.48 MPa and (2a, 50°) has −90.48 MPa.

Code: `src/mechanics/kirsch_analytic.cpp`; the BC set-up is in `tests/phase2/kirsch_common.hpp` and
`src/io/case_elastic2d.cpp`.

## 10. Error norms and the expected orders of convergence

With `e = u − u_h`, both errors are integrated element by element on the mesh with the 16-point
collapsed rule:

$$\|e\|_{L^2} = \left(\int_{\Omega_h} |u - u_h|^2\,dA\right)^{1/2},\qquad
\|e\|_E = \left(\int_{\Omega_h} (\sigma - \sigma_h)^T D^{-1} (\sigma - \sigma_h)\,dA\right)^{1/2}.$$

- The energy norm needs only the exact stress (no displacement gradient), and the exact stress is the
  quantity checked against the CSV.
- `(σ − σ_h)ᵀ D⁻¹ (σ − σ_h) = (ε − ε_h)ᵀ D (ε − ε_h)`.
- Both errors are divided by the corresponding norm of the exact solution (`‖u‖_L2`, `‖u‖_E`). That
  changes the constant, not the order.

**A priori estimates.** For a smooth solution and elements of degree p:

$$\|e\|_E \le C\,h^{p},\qquad \|e\|_{L^2} \le C\,h^{p+1}.$$

- The energy estimate is Céa's lemma: Galerkin is the best approximation in the energy norm, and the
  interpolation error of gradients is O(h^p).
- The L2 estimate adds one order by the Aubin-Nitsche duality argument.
- T3 (p = 1): **2 and 1**. T6 (p = 2): **3 and 2**.

**Assumptions behind them, and how each is met here.**
- **Smooth solution.** The domain excludes the hole, so the Kirsch field is analytic on it. The
  symmetry edges meet the hole and the outer square at right angles, which does not create a
  singularity for these boundary conditions.
- **Geometry approximated well enough.**
  - T6: isoparametric curved hole edges, O(h³) geometry, so the rates are kept.
  - T3: chords, O(h²) geometry. That is enough for both T3 rates.
- **Quadrature good enough** (section 6).
- **Quasi-uniform refinement.**
  - The Gmsh meshes at `-clscale 1, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16` keep the same grading (finer at the hole)
    while every size halves.
  - They are not nested, so h is measured, not assumed: `h = sqrt(area / n_cells)`.
  - The order is the least-squares slope of log(error) against log(h) over levels 1 to 4. Level 0 is
    computed and printed but is pre-asymptotic (section 13). The pairwise slopes are printed to show
    the asymptotic range.
  - It is the same `observed_order` function as phase 1.

Code: `src/mechanics/error_norms.cpp`, `tests/phase2/kirsch_convergence.cpp`.

## 11. The algorithm, step by step

1. Read the `.msh` file: nodes (m), cell blocks per physical group, triangles oriented counter-clockwise.
2. For each triangle in `domain`: evaluate shape functions at the quadrature points, the Jacobian,
   `B` and `D`, accumulate `K_e`, and push triplets.
3. For each edge in a traction group: map the 5 Gauss points to x, evaluate `t̄(x)`, and accumulate `f_e`.
4. Collect the Dirichlet dofs from the displacement groups (edges or points): a list of (dof, value).
5. Partition the dofs, then factorise and solve `K_ff u_f = f_f − K_fc g` with SimplicialLDLT, and
   compute the reactions.
6. Post-process: stresses at Gauss points, nodal averages (VTU), point stresses by inverse mapping,
   and the error norms against an exact field.

| P2-a | Patch test on a distorted mesh, T3 and T6, Dirichlet and traction versions, both plane conditions | Consistency: linear fields reproduced to round-off | wrong B or factor 2 in shear, wrong J⁻ᵀ, wrong T6 node order, wrong edge integration or normals, wrong D for either condition |
| P2-b | Bar in uniaxial tension | Traction load, minimal supports, reactions | edge-load scaling (factor of 2 from ξ ∈ [−1, 1]), point constraints, plane strain factor (1−ν²) |

## 13. What went wrong while building this phase, and how it was caught

1. **A claimed quadrature degree was wrong.** I first wrote that the collapsed Gauss rule with n × n
   points is exact to degree 2n − 1. The Jacobian factor (1 − u) adds one power of u, so the exact
   statement is degree 2n − 2 (section 6). I caught it while writing the exactness test, before any
   code ran. The test checks degree 2n − 2 for n = 1..5. Degree 6 (n = 4) is still more than the
   error norms need.
2. **The T6 L2 order missed its tolerance on the first run.**
   - The fit over levels 0 to 3 gave 3.25 (plane stress) and 3.27 (plane strain), outside 3 ± 0.15.
   - Diagnosis:
     - The pairwise orders were 3.53, 3.17 and 3.12, decreasing toward 3.
     - Integrating the error with 25 instead of 16 points per cell changed no reported digit, so the
       error norm itself was accurate.
     - The T6 energy order (1.99) and all T3 orders were within tolerance.
     - A scratch level 4 gave a pairwise order of 3.03.
   - Conclusion: the coarsest mesh, with about 8 elements on the quarter arc, is not yet in the
     asymptotic range for the L2 norm. Its error is relatively large because the a⁴/r⁴ terms near the
     hole are under-resolved, so the first slope is steeper than 3.
   - The fix changed the measurement after a result had been seen. Under the project's no-tuning rule
     that is the user's decision, so it was put to them, not made silently. With their approval,
     level 4 was added and the fit now uses levels 1 to 4 for both T3 and T6.
   - Result: 3.11 / 3.12 for T6 L2, and 1.97 / 1.97 for T6 energy. The change is recorded in the test
     comment and the README.
   - Lesson: decide beforehand which levels count as asymptotic, or include enough levels that one
     pre-asymptotic mesh cannot dominate a least-squares fit.

## 14. Limitations and the assumptions behind them

- **Linear, small-strain, isotropic, homogeneous.** One material per run; no body force.
- **Unit thickness.** Forces are per metre of depth. A real plate thickness only scales the reactions.
- **Triangles only (T3, T6).** No quadrilaterals.
- **T6 stiffness uses the 3-point rule.** It is not exact on curved elements; section 6 explains why it
  still converges optimally.
- **Point stresses come from a single element** (or the mean on a shared edge). There is no recovery
  scheme (SPR) beyond nodal averaging for plots.
- **The Kirsch model uses exact tractions.** It reproduces the infinite-plate solution and does not
  model a finite plate's edge effects. That is the point of the benchmark, but it is not the source's
  Abaqus set-up.
- **Mesh reproducibility.** The `.msh` files are generated by Gmsh 4.12.1 and committed. Another Gmsh
  version gives slightly different meshes and so slightly different numbers.

<details>
<summary>1. Exact tractions on a finite model</summary>

- The Kirsch field satisfies, inside the quarter: equilibrium, compatibility, the free hole and the
  symmetry conditions (u_θ ∝ sin 2θ vanishes on both axes).
- On the outer edges, the traction applied is `σ_exact n` itself.
- Linear elasticity with traction and displacement data has a unique solution, so the finite problem's
  exact solution is the Kirsch field restricted to the quarter. Any remaining difference is
  discretisation error, and it goes to zero with h at a measured rate (P2-f).
- With a uniform 140 MPa on y = 5a instead, the missing perturbation terms are of order
  `(3/2) S a²/r² ≈ 8 MPa` at r = 5a. They decay inward, but they are still several MPa at r = 4a,
  above the 2 MPa tolerance. The test would be checking the truncation, not the solver.
- Code: `tests/phase2/kirsch_common.hpp` (the traction functions), `src/mechanics/kirsch_analytic.cpp`.

</details>

<details>
<summary>2. Plane stress vs plane strain</summary>

- In 2D, with traction boundary conditions and no body force, the stress follows from equilibrium plus
  one compatibility equation. For an isotropic material in either plane condition, compatibility
  reduces to `∇²(σ_xx + σ_yy) = 0`, which contains no elastic constant.
- So the stress field depends on geometry and loads only (Michell's result). Kirsch's σ has no E or ν.
- The displacements need the constitutive law. They differ through μ and κ: `(3−ν)/(1+ν)` for plane
  stress, `3 − 4ν` for plane strain.
- The difference also shows in the strain energy and in the out-of-plane quantities: eps_zz ≠ 0 in
  plane stress, and σ_zz = ν(σ_xx + σ_yy) ≠ 0 in plane strain, which changes von Mises.
- In plasticity (phase 3) the two differ in stress too, because yield depends on σ_zz.

</details>

<details>
<summary>3. Mid-side nodes on the hole</summary>

- A T6 edge is the parabola through its three nodes. With the mid node on the circle, the parabola
  matches the arc to O(h³). With a straight edge (mid node on the chord), the gap is the chord sagitta,
  O(h²).
- The energy error then has a geometric part of order h^{3/2} (Strang-Berger) that the quadratic field
  cannot remove. Theory predicts that the T6 energy order would then stall near 1.5 instead of 2, and
  that the stress at the hole would converge slowly. tmfem does not test this case.
- Gmsh places mid nodes on the CAD curve. A reader test checks that every hole node lies on r = a to
  1e-12, on all five T6 levels. The measured T6 energy order of 1.97 confirms it.

</details>

<details>
<summary>4. The patch test</summary>

- If the exact solution is linear, it lies in the FE space, so Galerkin must return it exactly. The
  test checks that it does: interior displacements and constant stress to 1e-10.
- It is the practical form of the consistency condition, and with stability it gives convergence
  (Strang's patch test argument).
- A distorted mesh is needed because a regular mesh hides errors: symmetric cancellation can
  make a wrong Jacobian or shear term look right.
- An isoparametric element reproduces linear fields on any shape, because `Σ N_a = 1` and
  `Σ N_a x_a = x`. So `Σ N_a (A x_a + c) = A x + c` holds exactly even when the edges are curved. The
  field is linear in x, not in ξ.
- That alone is enough only with exact integration. With quadrature, the rule must also integrate
  `det J ∇_x N_a = adj(J)ᵀ ∇_ξ N_a` exactly, so that a constant stress gives the exact nodal forces.
  For a curved T6 this integrand has degree 2 (adj J linear, ∇_ξ N linear). The 3-point rule is exact
  for it, which is why the test passes (8e-14). A 1-point rule would fail it.
- What the test cannot catch: errors that only show for non-linear fields. Those are covered by the
  convergence test.

</details>

<details>
<summary>5. The factor 2 in engineering shear</summary>

- `γ_xy = ∂u_x/∂y + ∂u_y/∂x = 2 eps_xy`. It enters B as the row `[∂_y N_a, ∂_x N_a]` per node.
- With γ, the energy density is the plain dot product `σᵀ ε`, and the shear entry of D is G (not 2G).
- If B used ε_xy (½ of that row) with the same D, shear stiffness would be 4 times too small in the
  energy. If D used 2G with γ, it would be 2 times too large.
- Either error fails the patch test: the linear field in P2-a has non-zero shear
  (`∂u_x/∂y + ∂u_y/∂x = 1e-3 + 1e-3`), so the shear stress would come out wrong, and in the traction
  version the displacements too.
- Code: `src/mechanics/elasticity.cpp` (`strain_displacement` and the (3,3) entry of
  `elasticity_matrix`).

</details>
